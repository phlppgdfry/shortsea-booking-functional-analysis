# Five-minute story

## 0:00–0:30 — Scope
“This is an independent fictional case about moving a trailer to another sailing. Portal, EDI and booking agents currently follow inconsistent rules.”

## 0:30–1:15 — Discovery
“I prepared a stakeholder guide and simulated contradictory interview notes. Commercial wants key-account exceptions. Operations needs hard loading limits. In a real project I would validate these assumptions with the people doing the work.”

## 1:15–2:00 — Analysis
“One shared decision table prevents channels from recreating different policies. I modelled an amendment separately from its booking because accepted, declined and pending requests need an audit history.”

## 2:00–2:45 — Time and integration
“I found that exactly T-30 could mean both pending and expired. The corrected boundary closes requests and approvals at T-30. EDI receipt controls eligibility, while current status and time control whether execution is still possible.”

## 2:45–3:30 — Quality
“The checker runs the same rules as 27 executable examples. Additional tests cover approval revalidation and separate notification states. Tests do not prove the business assumptions or real transaction behaviour.”

## 3:30–4:15 — Delivery
“Applied means the booking commit succeeded and notifications were queued. Failed TOS delivery leaves that booking intact, alerts support and requires replay/reconciliation. The release plan includes a pilot, UAT, rollback and support training.”

## 4:15–5:00 — Limits and value
“The outcome targets are illustrative. I would establish a baseline and track incidents, channel mix and fee evidence. The case shows specifications and reasoning, not years of real workshop facilitation or logistics experience.”
